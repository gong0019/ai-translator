## SCOPE
ENGLISH → SIMPLIFIED CHINESE

## GRAMMAR
1. Use natural Simplified Chinese grammar: move every relative clause, participle phrase, and prepositional modifier in front of the noun it modifies, and break any modifier longer than about twelve characters into its own clause rather than stacking 的.
2. Put time, place, condition, cause, concession, and purpose adverbials before the main clause even when English places them last.
3. Render agentless English passives as active or subjectless Chinese clauses — `It is reported that` becomes 据报道, `can be seen` becomes 可以看出 — and keep 被 for an adverse action with a stated agent.
4. Turn English nominalizations back into verbs: `the implementation of the policy` becomes 实施该政策, `make an assessment of` becomes 评估. Drop empty category nouns such as 情况, 问题, 工作 when they carry no meaning.
5. Omit a subject pronoun whose referent is already clear, and repeat the noun instead of using a pronoun wherever two referents could be confused.
6. Split any sentence carrying two or more subordinate clauses into two or three shorter Chinese sentences separated by 。 or ；.
7. Make logical connectives explicit as Chinese pairs — `although` becomes 虽然…但…, `because` becomes 由于…因此… — and join coordinate items by juxtaposition instead of repeating 和.
8. Convert magnitudes and formats to Chinese convention: million to 百万, billion to 十亿, `percent` to %, dates to 年月日 order, and clock times to a 24-hour or 上午/下午 form.

## NAMES
Apply consistent transliteration to every personal name and reuse it on every repetition. Use established Chinese organization and place names for institutions, agencies, and geography. Leave no ordinary English residue outside protected spans.
